Should You Look Outside Your City for Renovation Vendors?

4 min read · Updated 2026

It's natural to default to the closest supplier or the first contractor who shows up in a search for your own city. But "closest" and "best value" aren't always the same thing — and in the GTA specifically, a 20-30 minute drive can put a meaningfully different set of options on the table.

Why prices can vary by area

Rent, labour costs, and local demand all factor into what a supplier or contractor charges — and those vary block by block, let alone city by city. A cabinet shop or contractor operating in a lower-overhead area can often price the same quality of work noticeably lower than one in a higher-demand neighbourhood, simply because their fixed costs are different.

This shows up clearly with renovation contractors: two crews with comparable experience and reviews can land 15-25% apart on the same job, purely based on where they're based and how far they typically travel for work.

Where this makes the most sense

  • Larger material orders — flooring, tile, and cabinetry in bulk. The per-unit savings from a lower-overhead supplier compound fast on a whole-home project.
  • Specialized trades — a supplier or contractor who focuses specifically on your project type (say, legal basement suites, or a particular style of cabinetry) is worth the drive even if a generalist is closer.
  • Community-connected trades — contractors who work heavily within a specific community often build deep expertise in that community's typical housing stock and renovation needs, which can be worth more than proximity.
Worth knowing: Scarborough, in particular, has a strong concentration of renovation contractors and suppliers serving the GTA's Chinese-speaking community — often at prices that are genuinely competitive once you account for what a comparable job costs closer to home. It's a reasonable place to check quotes even if you live in York Region.

What to weigh before you commit

Distance isn't free — it adds coordination time, and for contractors specifically, travel time sometimes gets factored into the quote or the schedule. A few practical questions worth asking before choosing a vendor further from home:

  • Does the price difference actually outweigh the extra coordination time, especially for a contractor who'll be on-site daily for weeks?
  • For material purchases, does the supplier deliver, or will you need to arrange transport yourself?
  • Is the quality and service level actually comparable, or just cheaper on paper — check reviews the same way you would locally.

The honest takeaway: don't rule out a supplier or contractor just because they're not in Richmond Hill, Markham, or Vaughan. A bit of extra distance is often a reasonable trade for meaningfully better pricing, especially on larger material orders where the savings scale with the size of your project.
